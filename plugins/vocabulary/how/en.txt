Vocabulary keeps the words you look up while reading, each with its sentence, to go over later. You find the list in Tools, newest first; choose a word to look it up again. The last row, Export, saves them for flash card apps such as Anki. The list keeps the newest 2,000 words: beyond that, the oldest go, so export them first to keep them all.

Flash cards, also in Tools, quiz you with spaced repetition: after Show answer you rate each card Again, Hard, Good or Easy, and that decides when it comes back. Make decks with Edit with your phone: scan the code with a phone in the same Wi-Fi, then type cards or add many at once: open a deck there, choose Many cards at once and pick a CSV, TSV or TXT file (a card on each line: front, back, example; UTF-8), paste a list, or bring an Anki deck (.apkg, exported with Support older Anki versions). 

Or use files on the SD card: put a CSV, TSV or TXT file for each deck in the folder Flashcards (Spanish.tsv makes the deck Spanish; one card on each line: front, back, example; saved as UTF-8; a first line may name the columns front, back, example). Choose Cards on the SD card in Flash cards, or let it happen when you open Flash cards. The decks stay on the reader with what you learnt, and a file is merged into its deck whenever it is new or changed: change the back or example of a card in the file and the card changes and keeps its progress; change the front text and it counts as a new card, the old one starts over (cards are told apart by the front text; spaces and capitals do not count); a line you delete does not delete the card. A file over 4 MiB or 20,000 lines is not read, and it must be saved as CSV UTF-8, not UTF-16. The first time, the reader makes the folder with a note on how it works.

In the settings, under Reading, Words looked up become cards puts each looked up word into the deck Looked up.
